Sprocket's Big Thunderstorm

a story about the scared feeling

Tap the arrow to begin →

Sprocket was playing outside when the sky turned dark. Clouds rolled in, thick and gray.

Then — CRACK! — thunder boomed. Sprocket's heart pounded fast, their legs froze in place, and their whole body felt cold and prickly.

Sprocket's thoughts darted fast: What was that?! Is it going to happen again?

Sprocket stopped and noticed: my heart is racing, my legs feel frozen. This feeling has a name — it's called SCARED.

Scared visits when something sudden or unknown happens. It's your body trying to keep you safe — even when there's nothing to be afraid of anymore.

So Sprocket tried something new: a slow brave breath, in through the nose, out through the mouth. Then, find your anchor.

Sprocket looked around and found their anchor — a trusted grown-up, right there. Their heart slowed down, just a little at a time.

So next time your heart races or your legs feel frozen, try what Sprocket tried. Notice it. Name it. Try a tool — a brave breath and finding your anchor both count.
