Sprocket's Fluttery Day

a story about the worried feeling

Tap the arrow to begin →

Sprocket had circled today on the calendar all week long. Today was finally the day to try Robot Park!

But right at the gate, Sprocket's tummy did something funny. Flutter, flutter, flutter — like a hundred little leaves swirling around inside.

Then Sprocket's thoughts started running too, tumbling over each other. What if the games are too hard? What if nobody wants to play? What if I get lost?

Sprocket stopped and took a breath. My tummy feels fluttery. My thoughts are racing. Oh — this feeling has a name. It's called WORRIED.

Worried visits every robot, and every kid, now and then. It shows up before new things, big things, exciting things. Feeling it doesn't mean anything is wrong.

So Sprocket tried something new: three slow breaths. In through the nose, nice and easy… and out, real slow. In… and out.

The fluttery feeling didn't vanish completely. But it grew quieter, softer, smaller. And Sprocket felt proud — not for feeling calm, but for trying anyway.

So next time your tummy flutters or your thoughts start to race, try what Sprocket tried. Notice it. Name it. Try a tool. You don't have to feel brave to be brave.
