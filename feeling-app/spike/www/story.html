Sprocket's Fluttery Day

a story about the worried feeling

Tap the arrow to begin →

Today was the day Sprocket got to try Robot Park for the very first time. Sprocket had been waiting all week!

But right before walking through the gate, Sprocket's tummy started to feel fluttery. Flutter, flutter, flutter — like tiny leaves blowing around inside.

Sprocket's mind started to race too. What if the games are too hard? What if nobody wants to play? What if I get lost?

Sprocket stopped and noticed: my tummy feels fluttery, my thoughts are going fast. This feeling has a name — it's called WORRIED.

Worried is a feeling every robot — and every kid — gets sometimes. It shows up before new things, big things, and exciting things too. It doesn't mean something is wrong.

So Sprocket tried something: three slow breaths. In through the nose… and out real slow. In… and out.

The fluttery feeling didn't disappear completely — but it got a little quieter. Sprocket noticed that, and felt proud. Not for feeling calm. For trying.

The next time your tummy feels fluttery or your thoughts start to race, try what Sprocket did: notice it, name it, try a tool. You don't have to feel brave to be brave.
