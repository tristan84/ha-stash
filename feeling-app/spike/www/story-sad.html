Sprocket's Rainy Afternoon

a story about the sad feeling

Tap the arrow to begin →

Sprocket had planned a picnic with a friend all week. Then the rain started tapping the window — tap, tap, tap — and the picnic was called off.

Sprocket's shoulders drooped low. Their chest felt heavy, like it was full of something slow and gray. Their eyes felt warm and prickly.

Sprocket's thoughts went quiet and slow: I don't feel like doing anything. It's just not fair.

Sprocket stopped and noticed: my chest feels heavy, my shoulders feel low. This feeling has a name — it's called SAD.

Sad visits when something we hoped for doesn't happen, or when we miss someone or something. It doesn't mean anything is wrong with you.

So Sprocket tried something new: wrap your arms around yourself in a cozy hug, and name one warm thing you love.

Sprocket held the hug and thought of warm things — a soft blanket, a friend's laugh. The heavy feeling didn't disappear, but it felt a little lighter to carry.

So next time your chest feels heavy or your shoulders feel low, try what Sprocket tried. Notice it. Name it. Try a tool — a cozy hug counts, even by yourself.
