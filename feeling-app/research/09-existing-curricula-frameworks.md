## Key Takeaways

1. **"The Zones of Regulation" is explicitly, strictly licensed — using
   its name or graphics in any digital product or app requires a licensing
   agreement.** This is unambiguous, direct from the rights-holder's own
   site: use of "The Zones of Regulation" name or Zones graphics in
   digital/physical products or apps is *not permitted without a licensing
   agreement*. There is an official companion app ecosystem and a "Digital
   Curriculum" product, meaning the rights-holder (Leah Kuypers /
   associated publisher) is **already actively commercializing the
   framework digitally themselves** — both a licensing risk (they enforce
   their trademark) and a competitive-landscape data point (a
   zones-based digital product already exists and is official).

2. **The Incredible 5-Point Scale has a more permissive, but still
   commercially unclear, posture.** The original authors (Kari Dunn Buron
   and co-authors, published via AAPC Publishing) explicitly welcome
   educators/therapists to **use the underlying strategies** and use
   **limited images** when sharing with parents/colleagues, and welcome
   use in research/class projects with citation — but this is permission
   for *educational/clinical practice use*, not a stated commercial
   app-licensing grant. An official commercial app ("Autism 5-Point Scale
   EP") already exists, built by a third-party developer using the
   named framework and authors — indicating commercial licensing **is**
   available, similar to the Zones ecosystem, just not confirmed at
   what terms.

3. **Both major named frameworks (Zones of Regulation, 5-Point Scale) are
   trademarked, commercially licensed intellectual property with existing
   official or authorized apps already in market.** This means: (a)
   building Sprocket's content taxonomy *around* either named framework
   without a license would be a real trademark/IP risk, not just a
   theoretical one — both are actively enforced/commercialized; (b) if
   this project ever wanted to license one of them, there's a clear
   precedent (existing third-party licensed apps) that licensing is
   obtainable, at unconfirmed cost/terms; (c) an **original taxonomy that
   is *inspired by* general emotion-regulation pedagogy (levels/scales of
   intensity, noticing-naming-managing progression) without using either
   framework's specific names, scale structure, or graphics** avoids this
   risk entirely and is well-supported independently by research note 01's
   five-stage model, which is generic instructional science, not any one
   company's trademarked content.

4. **Sesame Workshop's "Little Children, Big Challenges" / Breathe Think Do
   material (note 03) is a nonprofit educational initiative**, distinct in
   character from the two licensed clinical frameworks above — its content
   (the three-step Breathe/Think/Do strategy) is simple and widely
   publicly taught, though the specific Sesame characters/branding are of
   course still Sesame Workshop's IP. The underlying three-step *strategy
   concept* itself is generic pedagogy, not exclusive to Sesame.

## What This Changes In The Plan

- **Resolves Open Decision #4 toward: build an original taxonomy, not
  licensed on a named framework, for v1** — this is now a much clearer
  recommendation than research note 01 alone suggested, given the concrete
  licensing/trademark risk confirmed here for both Zones of Regulation and
  the 5-Point Scale. The five-stage notice→name→link→understand→manage
  structure (note 01) plus general "intensity levels/scale" pedagogy
  (common across the field, not exclusive to any one framework) can inform
  Sprocket's design without adopting anyone's trademarked name, graphics,
  or specific scale system. This is a recommendation for your sign-off —
  I'm not marking Open Decision #4 as locked, since content grounding is
  exactly the kind of decision you asked me to check with you on.
- **Rules out casually naming or visually referencing "Zones of
  Regulation" or "The Incredible 5-Point Scale" anywhere in Sprocket's
  content, marketing, or code/asset naming** without pursuing an actual
  license first — flagging this now, before any Phase 2 content design
  work starts, since it's cheap to avoid now and expensive to discover
  after content is built.
- **If, later, the project decides licensing a named framework is
  actually desired** (e.g., for credibility/parent trust), this note
  confirms it's a real, precedented option — both frameworks already have
  third-party licensed digital products — but that's a distinct future
  decision from the v1 "build original" recommendation above.
