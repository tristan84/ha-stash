## Competitor Summaries

### Mightier
- **What it is**: heart-rate biofeedback video games, played with a wearable
  heart-rate sensor, developed out of Boston Children's Hospital / Harvard
  Medical School. Ages 5–17 (games), clinically-oriented framing.
- **Mechanic**: the child's real-time heart rate affects gameplay difficulty;
  the game gets harder as heart rate rises, teaching the child to notice and
  actively bring their own physiological arousal down to keep playing —
  i.e., interoception made into direct, real-time gameplay feedback, not a
  taught concept.
- **Evidence**: cites its own randomized controlled trials — reduced
  outbursts (a 62% reduction figure is used in their own marketing —
  **[unverified as independent, this is a company-published figure]**),
  84% of parents reporting improvement in anger/frustration/aggression after
  3 months, and a same finding that combining Mightier with ABA
  outperformed ABA alone (81% vs 50% reporting improvement) in their cited
  research. Actively runs ongoing clinical trials (see ClinicalTrials.gov
  listings above) — this is a company still investing in evidence
  generation, not resting on old claims.
- **Business model**: appears to require a hardware sensor purchase/rental
  plus subscription — **[unverified, pricing model not directly confirmed
  in this pass]**.
- **Relevance to this project**: closest direct analog to the planned
  smartwatch "revving up" nudge feature (PLAN.md §6) — Mightier proves the
  core insight (real-time heart-rate feedback changes child behavior) works
  and has trial evidence, but does it as an in-the-moment *biofeedback game*
  requiring dedicated hardware and active play, not a passive nudge. Their
  approach to disclaiming/positioning while still citing real trial data is
  worth studying directly for Open Decision #9 (positioning language).

### Breathe, Think, Do with Sesame
- **What it is**: free-ish, Sesame Workshop (nonprofit) app for ages 2–5,
  part of their "Little Children, Big Challenges" resilience initiative.
  A monster character breathes, thinks of a plan, then tries it, across five
  interactive everyday-challenge scenarios, plus a standalone "Breathe with
  the Monster" activity.
- **Mechanic**: three-step named strategy (Breathe → Think → Do) taught
  through a character going through relatable problems, with light
  tap/interaction (pop bubbles, etc.) rather than deep gameplay.
- **Relevance to this project**: closest analog to the "Sprocket learns
  alongside the child" premise — a character modeling the skill rather than
  a human authority figure teaching it. Skews younger (2–5) than this
  project's likely age range (Open Decision #2), and is much lighter-weight
  content-wise (five scenarios total) than what this project's calm-moment
  games + Help Me mode implies. Good reference for tone, not for scope.

### Smiling Mind
- **What it is**: free, ad-free, nonprofit (Australian) mindfulness app,
  ages 3+ through adult, plus a separate curriculum-aligned schools program.
  Over a decade of operation.
- **Mechanic**: structured, age-banded meditation/mindfulness sessions;
  offline access; progress tracking; explicitly "evidence-informed"
  built by a team including psychologists.
- **Business model**: free (nonprofit-funded) — notably the one competitor
  in this scan with **no monetization pressure at all** on the product
  design, which likely explains the "no ads, offline access" combination
  this project also wants (Principle 1).
- **Relevance to this project**: strongest precedent for "no ads, privacy-respecting,
  free-or-low-cost, offline-capable" being viable for a kids' wellbeing
  app long-term — proof this isn't just an idealistic constraint, one
  competitor has run on it for 12+ years. Doesn't address special-needs
  kids or body-effects/interoception specifically — it's general mindfulness,
  not feelings-vocabulary or body-mind-effect education.

### Otsimo
- **What it is**: subscription special-education app suite (AAC + learning
  activities) for autism, ADHD, Down syndrome, and other needs. Built with
  child psychologists/educators, uses adaptive difficulty ("auto-level").
  Multiple awards (Mom's Choice, HundrED Global Collection 2020–2022).
- **Mechanic**: broad activity library (communication/AAC, vocabulary,
  social skills, fine motor via puzzles/tracing) rather than a single
  focused mechanic.
- **Business model**: freemium, **no ads even in the free tier** — premium
  tier priced around $13–20/month depending on billing term.
- **Relevance to this project**: closest competitor in *audience* (autism,
  ADHD, other special needs) but broader in scope (general special-ed
  skills, not feelings-specific) and not focused on interoception/body-mind
  effects or a "Help me" real-time mode. Its adaptive-difficulty
  ("auto-level") approach and no-ads-even-in-free-tier stance are both
  directly relevant precedents. Pricing gives a market data point for Open
  Decision territory (monetization isn't in PLAN.md's open decisions list
  yet — worth adding, see note below).

### Moshi
- **What it is**: London-based, VC-funded (Accel-led $12M Series B) kids'
  sleep/mindfulness audio app, ages up to ~10. 85+ hours of audio stories,
  meditations, music; themes include empathy, mindfulness, self-regulation.
- **Business model**: subscription, ~$40/year, free 7-day trial. Grew
  subscriber base 7x in 2019 to 100K.
- **Relevance to this project**: least directly overlapping competitor —
  Moshi is audio-content/sleep-focused, not an interactive skills app, and
  isn't targeted at special-needs kids specifically. Useful mainly as a
  monetization/funding-viability data point (a kids' wellbeing app in this
  broad space raised real venture funding and has real subscriber traction)
  and as a caution: it's the one competitor here that is VC-funded and
  subscription-first, which sits in some tension with a privacy-first,
  no-tracking, no-ads posture if this project ever seeks similar funding —
  worth being explicit about that tradeoff if/when fundraising is discussed.

## Cross-Cutting Observations

- **No competitor in this scan combines**: (a) explicit body-effects +
  mind-effects framing, (b) a companion character who is *also learning*
  (vs. an authority-figure character or none), (c) special-needs-inclusive
  design, and (d) a distinct one-tap real-time "Help me" mode, in one
  product. Breathe Think Do has (b) partially; Otsimo has (c); Mightier has
  a real-time mode but as active biofeedback gameplay, not a calm "here are
  your practiced tools" surface. This is a genuine gap, not just a
  differentiated angle — worth stating plainly rather than hedging.
- **Ads-free is normal, not a differentiator, in this specific competitive
  set**: Smiling Mind (free/nonprofit) and Otsimo (freemium) are both
  already ad-free even on free tiers. Privacy-first (Principle 1) should be
  understood as table stakes for this category, not something that alone
  sets this app apart — the differentiation is in content/approach (body-mind
  framing, Sprocket, Help Me), not in the privacy posture itself.
- **A clinical-evidence competitor exists (Mightier)** — if this project
  ever wants to make efficacy claims, Mightier's approach (fund real RCTs,
  cite them plainly, keep marketing framed around "a tool" not "a cure") is
  a template to study for Open Decision #9, more so than any general
  marketing-copy guidance.

## What This Changes In The Plan

- **Confirms a real gap in the market** for the specific combination this
  project is planning (body+mind effects, co-learning companion,
  special-needs-inclusive, dedicated Help Me mode) — supports moving
  forward rather than a signal to pivot the concept.
- **Adds a missing open decision**: PLAN.md §9 doesn't currently have a
  monetization/business-model open decision, and this scan surfaces three
  different viable models (nonprofit/free, freemium-subscription,
  VC-funded-subscription) with real tradeoffs against Principle 1
  (privacy-first, no ads/trackers). **Recommend adding this to PLAN.md §9
  as a new open decision** — flagged here rather than added unilaterally,
  since it's a new decision, not something the plan already scoped.
- **Otsimo's adaptive difficulty ("auto-level") is a relevant prior-art
  pattern** worth a look during Phase 2 content design, for personalizing
  calm-moment game difficulty per child.
- **Mightier's positioning approach is now the concrete reference case**
  for Open Decision #9 (positioning language) — cite it directly rather
  than working that language from scratch.
