## Key Takeaways

1. **Interoception is the mechanism, not just a metaphor.** Interoception —
   the ability to sense the body's internal state — is treated in the
   literature as a specific, trainable perceptual skill that sits upstream of
   emotional awareness and emotion regulation, not just a nice framing for
   "body effects." The MABT (Mindful Awareness in Body-Oriented Therapy)
   model frames interoceptive awareness as *necessary* for identifying the
   physiological processes underlying feelings.

2. **There's a specific, teachable five-stage progression**, per GriffinOT's
   summary of interoception-curriculum practice: **noticing → naming →
   linking to feelings → understanding impact → managing.** This maps
   closely onto the app's stated goal ("notice, name, understand, and
   manage feelings") and is a strong candidate for the app's core taxonomy
   structure — it wasn't designed for this app, but it fits almost exactly.

3. **Interoception-based programs have direct evidence in autistic children,
   specifically for emotion regulation**, not just body awareness. A 2022
   study (PMC9045986) found a structured interoception program produced
   statistically significant improvements in both interoceptive awareness
   and emotion regulation in autistic children, and a 2020 pilot study
   (Journal of OT, Schools & Early Intervention) supports feasibility of an
   interoception-based intervention for this population. A 7-week program
   also improved emotion regulation across children with diverse diagnoses
   in a special-education setting (per GriffinOT's summary — **[unverified]**
   as I did not locate the primary source for this specific 7-week claim,
   only the secondary practitioner summary).

4. **Interoception connects to *recognizing other people's* emotions too,
   not just your own.** One ScienceDirect study found interoceptive
   awareness mitigates deficits in recognizing emotional tone of voice
   (prosody) in autistic individuals — suggesting body-awareness training
   could have a secondary benefit for social-emotional skills beyond
   self-regulation. Interesting for later content design, not urgent for v1.

5. **The field is still active and evolving.** A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis
   (Frontiers in Psychiatry) on interoception in autism spectrum disorder
   indicates this is a live research area with ongoing synthesis work, not a
   settled, decade-old consensus — reasonable confidence, but not to be
   treated as beyond revision.

## What This Changes In The Plan

- **Strong support for Principle 5** ("body and mind, always both") — this
  isn't just a nice design choice, it reflects how the research frames
  emotion regulation as mechanistically dependent on interoceptive
  (body-sensation) awareness.
- **The five-stage model (notice → name → link → understand → manage) is a
  good candidate for the app's core learning taxonomy** — added as an
  input to Open Decision #4 in PLAN.md §9 (content grounding). This is
  *not* the same as adopting Zones of Regulation or another named
  commercial/clinical framework wholesale — it's a general instructional
  sequence, not a licensed curriculum, so it likely carries less licensing
  risk than adopting a named framework outright. Still needs a check
  against research item 9 (framework licensing) before finalizing.
- **Supports body-first ordering within a single feeling's content**: teach
  the body sensation before naming the feeling, rather than starting from
  the word. Worth prototyping both orders in Phase 2 content design rather
  than assuming.
- **Autism-specific evidence directly supports doing this work for special-needs
  kids specifically**, not just adapting a neurotypical-kids product — this
  is evidence *for* the project's premise, not just adjacent research.
