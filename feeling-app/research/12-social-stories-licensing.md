## Key Takeaways

1. **"Social Story™"/"Social Stories™" is Carol Gray's trademarked term for
   stories built to her specific method**, not a generic industry term she
   has no claim over. Multiple sources (her own site, secondary summaries)
   consistently describe it as trademarked and attributed specifically to
   her and "Team Social Stories."

2. **There is genuine ambiguity on trademark registration status**, worth
   flagging rather than glossing over: one non-authoritative legal-forum
   source describes it as an "unregistered trademark," while Carol Gray's
   own materials use the ™ symbol (which itself typically signals a
   claimed-but-not-necessarily-federally-registered mark, as opposed to
   ®). This distinction matters practically — an unregistered/common-law
   trademark can still be enforceable, just with different remedies than a
   registered one — but this research pass cannot resolve the exact legal
   status. **[ambiguous — registration status not independently confirmed
   against USPTO records]**

3. **The specific methodology is separately, unambiguously copyrighted.**
   Her "Social Stories™ 10.2" and newer "10.4" criteria documents, and
   associated workbooks, carry explicit copyright notices ("may not be
   duplicated via any means electronic or otherwise without prior written
   permission of the author"). This is the clearer, stronger protection
   here — independent of the trademark-registration ambiguity above, the
   **specific written criteria and materials are copyrighted content**,
   and reproducing or closely implementing them without permission is a
   more clear-cut risk than the trademark question.

4. **Descriptive/generic use of the lowercase phrase may be lower-risk than
   using it as a product/feature name.** One (non-authoritative) legal
   discussion found in this pass suggested that using "social stories" in
   a purely descriptive sense (i.e., "this is a type of story that teaches
   a social situation," not branding a specific product as *the* Social
   Story™ method) is more defensible than using the term as a trademark
   identifier. This mirrors the general trademark principle that
   descriptive/generic use is treated differently from source-identifying
   use — but this is general trademark principle applied by a non-lawyer
   research pass, not a legal opinion for this specific case.

5. **The underlying idea — a short, first-person, supportive narrative
   describing a specific situation, expectation, or skill for a child —
   is a widely-used general concept in the autism-support field**,
   independent of Carol Gray's specific ten-criteria method. This is
   analogous to what note 09 found for Zones of Regulation and the
   5-Point Scale: the *general pedagogical concept* is not proprietary,
   even where a *specific named, criteria-bound method* is protected.

## What This Changes In The Plan

- **Extends note 09's recommendation, rather than changing it.** Sprocket's
  content should not be branded or marketed using the "Social Story™" /
  "Social Stories™" name, and should not implement content strictly
  against Carol Gray's copyrighted 10.2/10.4 criteria documents, without a
  license. This is consistent with — and reinforces — Open Decision #4's
  research-backed recommendation (original taxonomy, not licensed on a
  named framework) already in PLAN.md §9: the same reasoning that ruled
  out Zones of Regulation and the 5-Point Scale for v1 applies here.
- **The general concept (short supportive narratives describing a specific
  situation/expectation) remains usable** as inspiration for calm-moment
  game content or Help Me flow scripting, so long as it's built as
  original content and not named or structured as "Social Stories" per
  her specific method — same "inspired by generic pedagogy, not a named
  licensed framework" pattern as note 09's recommendation.
- **No change to any locked decision** — this closes out the research gap
  under Open Decision #4/#13, and the recommendation is consistent with
  what's already flagged as pending your sign-off in PLAN.md §9.
